Custom backgrounds

Design spec · 2026-09-03 · branch worktree-custom-backgrounds · status: awaiting Shazwan's review

Two things. A workspace background (the Gmail theme idea): the paper canvas behind every screen becomes an image the signed-in person picked, seen only by them. A profile cover: a picture across the top of one person's profile page, seen by everyone who opens that profile, which fades and blurs out into the page instead of filling it.

1. What I understood (please confirm)

FeatureWorkspace backgroundProfile cover
What it isFull-page wallpaper behind the cards, like Gmail themes.Banner at the top of the profile page only. Not full page.
Who sets itEach person, for themselves.The employee, on their own profile. HR can remove any cover.
Who sees itOnly the person who set it. Nobody else's screen changes.Everyone who views that profile, including the slim card a restricted viewer gets.
Where it showsEvery app screen: dashboard, boards, timesheets, all of it. Not the login page, not the wizard, not embedded panels.Profile screen only. Not the directory tile, not the org chart, not the header avatar.
SourceSix built-in presets, or upload your own photo.Upload your own photo only. No presets.
Bottom edgen/a, fills the viewport.Sharp at the top. From roughly 40% down it blurs, and from 70% down it fades, so by the bottom it is plain canvas. The identity card overlaps it.
Assumptions I made without asking. These are the ones that change the work if wrong.
  1. The cover is public inside the company. Anyone who can open the profile sees it. If covers should be visible only to the owner, the profile cover becomes a personal decoration and the spec shrinks to one column.
  2. The workspace background lives in Account & security as a new "Appearance" card, with a shortcut in the account menu. There is no personal settings page today, and that screen is the only one that is about you rather than the company.
  3. Cards stay opaque white on a wallpaper. Only the header and text that sits directly on the canvas get a frosted paper backplate. Making cards translucent would hurt the ledger's readability everywhere for a decoration.
  4. No tenant on/off switch. Both are personal choices, they change nothing for anyone else, and a switch in the Features card can be added in an hour if a client asks.
  5. Presets are CSS gradients, not photos. No image files to license, nothing to ship in public/, and they load instantly. Real photos can replace them later without touching the data model.

2. Workspace background

The shell keeps its shape. The dark sidebar is untouched: it is the one heavy element and stays the anchor. The header stops being opaque canvas and becomes a frosted band so the picture reads through it (this is the Gmail move). Cards stay white. The page title and any subline that sits on bare canvas get a translucent paper backplate so ink never sits on a photograph.

How the layers stack

body.uj-has-wallpaper
  .uj-wallpaper        position:fixed; inset:0; z-index:0; background-image:url(…) | preset gradient
                       background-size:cover; background-position:center
  .uj-wallpaper::after  the dim: canvas at 30% (soft) or 55% (strong) over the image
  .uj-sidebar          unchanged, z-index above the wallpaper
  .uj-header           background: color-mix(in srgb, var(--canvas) 72%, transparent) + blur(14px) inline
  .uj-hd-fade          unchanged
  .uj-head-stack       gets .uj-plate on the title block: canvas 74% + blur(14px), radius 10px
  .uj-card             unchanged, opaque white

Only three rules change when a wallpaper is on, all scoped under body.uj-has-wallpaper: the wallpaper layer, the header background, the title backplate. Nothing moves; no screen needs editing. The blur goes inline the way .uj-hd-fade already does, because Lightning CSS drops the unprefixed backdrop-filter (see the comment in app.css).

Mobile: the wallpaper is position:fixed so it does not repaint on scroll. The dock at the bottom stays opaque. Under 520px the dim steps up one level automatically, because small screens have less card and more bare canvas showing.

  • Selecting is saving. One click per tile, one POST, the wallpaper behind the card swaps at once. A form with a Save button would make people pick, scroll, save, scroll back to compare. Gmail does the same.
  • Upload replaces the previous upload and becomes the selected tile. One personal photo per account, not a gallery. Picking a preset afterwards keeps the photo tile so they can switch back without re-uploading.
  • Dim is the only knob. It exists because a bright photo behind a page of leave rows is unreadable, and because a single value would be wrong for half the photos. None / Soft / Strong, default Soft.
  • Account menu gets one row, "Background", under "My profile", linking to this card. Discovery, nothing more.

3. Profile cover

Revised 2026-09-03 after review: the cover is a full-width backdrop from the top of the main area that fades into the wallpaper, not a boxed image; the page title has no plate. The mockup below predates this.

The cover is not a hero. It is a strip of colour and place at the top of a person's record that dissolves into the ledger below. The sharp image holds the top 40%. Over the next 30% a blurred copy of the same image takes over. From 70% down everything fades to canvas. The identity card rides up over the lower third with a soft shadow, so it reads as sitting on top of the picture rather than under it.

Profile, own record, desktop. The cover is 200px tall. "Change cover" shows for the owner and for HR (as "Remove cover"). The rest of the page is exactly as it is today.

The fade, exactly

.uj-cover            position:relative; height:200px (150px ≤ 640px); overflow:hidden
.uj-cover-img        the photo, object-fit:cover, object-position:center
                     mask: linear-gradient(to bottom, #000 35%, transparent 80%)
.uj-cover-blur       same photo, filter:blur(18px), transform:scale(1.08) to hide blurred edges
                     mask: linear-gradient(to bottom, transparent 30%, #000 60%, transparent 88%)
                     (both reach fully transparent before the box ends, so there is never a hard bottom edge)
.uj-cover-change     top-right; canvas 78% + blur(10px) pill; owner and HR only
identity .uj-card    margin-top:-64px (−54px on phones); shadow 0 8px 24px -12px rgba(38,37,30,.28)
avatar               4px white ring so the initials disc separates from the photo

Two copies of one image, two masks. No JavaScript, no canvas work, and the blur is a filter on an element (not a backdrop-filter) so the Lightning CSS problem does not apply. The blurred copy is also what makes the fade look like depth rather than a gradient sticker: as the sharp image thins out the soft one carries the colour down, then lets go.

  • Who sees the control. Owner: "Change cover" (upload replaces). Owner with a cover: also "Remove". HR on someone else's profile: "Remove cover" only, for moderation. Anyone else: no control, no hint.
  • Restricted viewers (the slim card) get the cover on top of that card at 120px. It is part of who the person is, like their name.
  • No cropping tool. Centre crop, 2000px max edge after compression. A vertical focal-point picker is the first upgrade if people complain heads get cut off; the column for it (cover_focus, 0–100) is deliberately not added now.

4. Decisions I made silently

DecisionChosenWhy
Where the user preference livesNew users.appearance json column, cast to array: {"wallpaper": "preset:dawn" | "upload", "wallpaper_path": "…", "dim": "soft"}Mirrors users.dashboard_prefs. One column, one migration, room for a future theme key without another migration.
Where the cover livesNew employees.cover_path string, nullableThe cover belongs to the employee record (what others see), not the login (what you see). employees.photo already exists but is unused and named for an avatar; leaving it alone avoids a future collision.
Storage diskpublic disk, wallpapers/{user}/… and covers/{employee}/…, hashed filenamesSame as the tenant logo (AdminController). Served by the web server, no PHP per image. Filenames are unguessable; a background is not a payslip.
Image limitsjpeg/png/webp, 5 MB, then ImageCompressor::compress (2000px max edge, q82)Existing helper, existing rule set from the knowledge bank uploads.
Replace deletes old fileYes, on upload and on RemoveShared hosting disk is finite; nothing references the old file.
Wallpaper on login and wizardNoNot signed in on one, not settled in on the other.
Embedded panels ($embed)No wallpaperThey already sit inside a card on the parent screen.
Dark text on light photosHandled by the backplate and dim, not by switching the type to whiteOne ink colour system-wide is the design; the plate keeps that true.
Preset setSix gradients: Dawn, Dusk, Paper, Moss, Slate, SandWarm and cool, light and dark, one that is barely a change (Paper) for people who want texture without a picture.

5. Data, routes, permissions

Migrations

users:      appearance json nullable, after dashboard_prefs
employees:  cover_path string(200) nullable, after photo

Routes (inside the existing authed /app group)

Method · pathNameDoesWho
POST /app/account/appearanceaccount.appearanceFields: wallpaper (none | preset:<key> | upload), dim (none | soft | strong), optional file photo. Saves, returns 204 for fetch or redirects back.Any signed-in user, own row only
POST /app/account/appearance/photo/deleteaccount.appearance.photo.destroyDeletes the upload, falls back to wallpaper: none if it was selected.Same
POST /app/employees/{employee}/coveremployees.cover.updateUpload replaces cover.Owner only
POST /app/employees/{employee}/cover/deleteemployees.cover.destroyRemoves cover and file.Owner, or anyone who passes the profile's existing $canEdit
Tenant check is explicit. Route-model binding is not tenant-scoped here (SubstituteBindings runs before ResolveTenant). Both cover routes abort_unless($employee->tenant_id === $tenant->id, 404) before anything else, same as the other employee routes.

Rendering

  • layouts/app.blade.php reads auth()->user()->appearance, sets uj-has-wallpaper on the shell and renders the .uj-wallpaper div with an inline background-image (upload URL or preset gradient from config/amanahku.php → wallpaper_presets). Server-rendered, so no flash of plain canvas.
  • screens/profile.blade.php renders the cover block above the identity band when $p->cover_path is set, or the dashed invitation when $isOwn and nothing is set.
  • Appearance card in screens/security.blade.php. Tile clicks POST via fetch and swap the wallpaper div's inline style in place; the file input submits the same way. No full-page reload.

7. Tests

  • AppearanceTest: preset saves; unknown preset rejected (422); dim saves; upload stores under wallpapers/{user}, replaces and deletes the previous file, sets wallpaper: upload; delete-photo removes file and resets selection; oversize and non-image rejected; the layout renders uj-has-wallpaper and the URL for a user with a wallpaper, and not for one without; embed and login render none.
  • ProfileCoverTest: owner uploads, cover renders on own profile and on a colleague's view and on the slim card; other employee cannot upload (403); HR can remove someone else's cover; other tenant's employee id returns 404; remove deletes the file; invitation shows only to the owner with no cover.
  • Storage faked with Storage::fake('public') throughout.

8. Not in this round

  • Photo presets (needs licensed images). Data model already fits them.
  • Focal-point / crop tool for the cover.
  • Tenant switch to disable backgrounds.
  • Dark theme. "Appearance" is named so it can hold that later, but it is a separate spec.
  • Text or vignette options beyond Dim.
