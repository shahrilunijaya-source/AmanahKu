## Requirement
When a public holiday falls on the next working day (e.g. Malaysia Day on Wed 16 Sep), the clock-out on the eve (Tue 15 Sep, afternoon) shows a full-screen 'Happy holiday!' greeting, curated for that specific holiday, not a generic message.

## Why
Small moments of delight at the end of the day; makes the app feel human and reinforces the culture work in CR-13/14.

## Scope
1. Source: the public-holiday calendar already shown in My calendar (national + Kelantan/state holidays as configured by HR). Trigger = clock-out on the last working day before a holiday (also handles long weekends: Friday eve for a Monday holiday) and, for multi-day holidays, once only.
2. Curated content per holiday maintained by HR/Admin in a simple table: holiday name, greeting text (EN + BM), illustration/colour theme, optional short line about the holiday. Examples: Malaysia Day, 'Selamat Hari Malaysia! [TRANSCRIPTION GAP: emoji in source PDF] Rehat, jumpa Khamis.'; Hari Raya, 'Selamat Hari Raya Aidilfitri, maaf zahir dan batin'; Deepavali, 'Happy Deepavali, may light win'; CNY, 'Gong Xi Fa Cai'; Christmas, Merdeka, Awal Muharram, Thaipusam, Wesak, Agong's birthday, state holidays. Fallback generic greeting if no entry.
3. Prompt shows after clock-out succeeds: greeting, holiday name and date, 'See you on <next working day>', small confetti/theme animation (respects reduce-motion), dismiss button. Also delivered as a push notification for staff who clock out via mobile. Shown once per person per holiday.
4. Staff on leave that day or not clocked in still receive the push notification at 5:30 PM.
5. Optional: same greeting card posted to the Dashboard on the eve for everyone to react to (CR-13 banner component).

## Acceptance
1. Clock out on 15 Sep 2026, Malaysia Day greeting appears with 'See you on Thursday 17 Sep'.
2. Clock out on 14 Sep, no greeting.
3. HR edits the Malaysia Day text, next clock-out shows the new text.
4. Holiday with no curated entry, generic 'Happy holiday!' shown.
5. Clock out twice on the eve (correction), greeting shown once.
